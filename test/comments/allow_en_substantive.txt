The proposal to replace chronological feeds with engagement-ranked algorithms is a mistake
because it optimizes for time-on-site rather than information quality, and we can see why
through three concrete mechanisms rather than appeals to intuition. First, engagement ranking
amplifies whatever provokes reaction, and provocation correlates weakly with accuracy: a
correction of last week's rumor rarely outperforms the rumor itself in click-through, so the
ranking structurally buries the people doing unpaid fact-checking work. Second, it creates
incentive distortion on the authoring side. When visibility depends on predicted reaction
instead of subscriber choice, authors rationally shift from careful analysis toward outrage
framing, and my own observation across three hobby forums that adopted ranking confirms the
drift: long technical threads thinned within months while screenshot-and-react posts
multiplied. Third, subscriber control already exists and works - mailing lists remain
readable precisely because the user, not a model, decides what arrives. The better path is
opt-in boosts plus transparent ranking labels so readers can audit what they are shown.
